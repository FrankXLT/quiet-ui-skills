1.0 Fundamental Cramér-Rao Phase Variance Bounds

Coherent aperture synthesis across kilometer-scale baselines requires distributing a common local oscillator whose integrated phase error remains strictly below a fiftieth of a carrier wavelength[1]. In conventional RF coaxial distribution, diurnal thermal expansion of the dielectric core induces slow phase wander exceeding 45 picoseconds per kelvin-kilometer.

σ_φ²(τ) ≥ [ 8 · π² · f_c² · SNR · B_n · τ ]⁻¹ + S_thermal(ΔT, L) (Eq. 1.1)

By embedding bidirectional Dense Wavelength Division Multiplexing (DWDM) at 1490 nm downstream and 1310 nm upstream over a single dispersion-shifted SMF-28e+ strand, the round-trip propagation delay τ_rt is continuously estimated using Digital Dual-Mixer Time Difference (DDMTD) phase detectors[2].

2.0 Chromatic Dispersion & Sagnac Asymmetry Compensation

Because the refractive group indices n_g(λ₁) and n_g(λ₂) differ slightly across the 180 nm spectral separation, first-order asymmetry calibration must apply the static fiber dispersion coefficient α prior to closing the helper VCXO loop:

δ_m,s = ½ · [ (τ_rt - ε_tx - ε_rx) · (1 + α)⁻¹ ] + Δτ_Sagnac(Ω_E, A_proj) (Eq. 2.1)
